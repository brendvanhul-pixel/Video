# Say what moves: explainer film script

Nov 19, 2026 · @Brend

Recap film for the Say what moves e-learning. Source: Say\_what\_moves.docx (7 screens).

## 1. Treatment

**Purpose.** Recap after the course. The viewer has already met the project controller, the sales director, the harbour project manager and Nadia, so the film can move fast and skip the set-up a trailer would need.

**Audience.** Office staff: project control, finance, sales support, planning. People whose work arrives as requests from several directions and whose shortcuts are invisible, because nothing on a desk visibly breaks.

**Playback context.** LMS with sound. Assume a share of viewers watch muted on a laptop in an open-plan office, so every outcome and the key sentence also appear as on-screen text.

**Length: 90 seconds, not 120.** The course holds one core story (Screens 1 to 3: the director's request, asking the owner, sending Nadia a plan) and two follow-up stories (Nadia as the asker on Screen 4, the week that still does not fit on Screen 6). The core story runs comfortably in 90 seconds. Getting to 120 would mean pulling in one of the follow-ups, and each of those carries its own rule. That makes a film with two messages, and the second one blurs the first. The follow-ups make a better second film (see the cut list).

**Behaviour change.** When new work lands on a full week, the person says which work gives way and asks the owner of that work before it moves.

**Controlling idea.** On a full week every yes moves someone else's work, so the owner of that work has to be asked before it moves, not told after.

**Shape: follow the object, with one rewind.** The film follows the harbour costing, drawn as a tile on a week rail, from Thursday 15:00 to Nadia's "OK, go." The lesson of the course is invisible in an office: it is about who knows what, and when. A physical object that gets shoved off the end of a full rail gives that a body the viewer can watch. The rewind does a second job: it compresses the four wrong answers of Screen 1 into one gesture, so the film spends its time on the right answer and its consequence instead of on the failures.

**Hook.** Sound and image: a tile shoved off a full rail, two beats of silence, a distant thud. **Ending.** Callback to the first frame, with the opposite result. **Tone.** Wry. The course already carries the weight; office viewers get the joke of four answers that each go wrong in a new way. **VO.** Sparse narrator plus one in-scene character line.

## 2. Cut list

- **Screen 4, Nadia in the corridor ("Ideally both").** When the asker also owns the work that moves, the rule shifts: you tell her what moves and give her a deadline to swap it back. Strong material, different rule. Second film.
- **Screen 6, the week that still does not fit.** Every trade-off was agreed, nobody agreed to the total. This is its own message and the best spine for a second film, paired with Screen 4.
- **Screen 5, the ten-minute favour.** Two ideas in one screen: estimating before promising, and free time versus claimed time. The estimation point is a separate skill. The sort rule repeats what the film already shows with the director.
- **Screen 7, the if-then plan and the email to the manager.** Personal reflection works in the course and dies on screen. The film cannot make anyone write their sentence.
- **The Sarens review quote on naming priorities.** Abstract. The rail shows the same thing in two seconds.
- **Screen 3, option C (going to the director's manager).** A third path dilutes the clean puzzle-versus-plan comparison.
- **Screen 2 distractors (Friday admin, "later", "after the tender", asking the director).** The course teaches these. The film shows only the finished sentence.
- **Most of the exact times.** Kept only as on-screen clocks where they carry the story (15:00, 15:20, 16:10, 18:00, 7:30). None are read out except the opening.
- **The 17:00 costing deadline and Monday 10:00 transport detail.** Shown in Nadia's plan as tiles, not spoken.

**Proposed second film:** "Nobody agreed to the total", 60 to 75 seconds, built from Screens 4 and 6. Different shape (countdown to Thursday's bridge report suits it), different ending.

## 4. VO notes

**Word count.** Narrator 89 words, controller line 27 words, total 116. At 2.3 words per second that is about 50 seconds of speech in a 90-second film, which leaves the opening, the rewind and the ending to picture and sound.

**Timed read per row.** 0:10 row: 20 words, about 9 s in a 12 s slot. 0:22 row: 12 words, 5 s in 14 s (the takes need room). 0:36 row: 16 words, 7 s in 8 s, read it straight into the rewind. Controller line: 27 words, about 12 s in 15 s, conversational pace. 0:59 row: 11 words, 5 s in 9 s, pause after "Good." 1:08 row: 11 words, 5 s in 6 s. 1:14 row: 19 words, 8 s in 11 s.

**Narrator.** Dry, warm, unhurried. Someone who has watched this happen on a lot of Thursdays. No coaching voice, no upward lift at line ends. Clear for non-native English listeners across Sarens regions: neutral accent, no fast contractions.

**Controller.** A second, different voice, cast to match the character design. Plain and calm, not rehearsed. The line should sound like a person working it out at the desk, with a small breath before "I'll check that".

**Recording note.** Record the controller line in one take, start to finish. The on-screen gaps are timed to its natural rhythm, so animation follows the read, not the other way round.

## 5. Animator notes

**Hero shape: the week rail.** A flat horizontal track with tiles sitting edge to edge. Each tile carries its owner's colour, and each owner wears that colour: harbour PM red, director a second accent, Nadia a third, the bridge PM a fourth, the controller's own admin neutral grey. The new request is always the white tile. Keep the rail the same length, height and tile size in every scene so the viewer reads it as the same object.

**Transitions.** Element-driven throughout. The white tile swipes between the four takes. The rewind runs everything backwards on the rail rather than cutting. The second message bubble wipes the first.

**Sync points that must land exactly.**

- 0:04 the thud, after two clean beats of silence. If music or room tone covers the gap, the hook fails.
- 0:08 piano note on the title flip.
- 0:44 to 0:59 each bracket in the on-screen sentence clicks into place on the matching words of the controller's read.
- 1:20 the brighter chime on "OK, go."
- 1:27 the final click replaces the opening thud. Use the same slide sound so the callback reads.

**Muted check.** Watched without sound, the viewer still gets: four labelled answers that each go wrong, the finished sentence, the PM's reply, the two messages with their replies, and the end line. Test a silent cut before sign-off.
