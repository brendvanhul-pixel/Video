# Say what moves: explainer film script

Nov 19, 2026 · @Brend

Recap film for the Say what moves e-learning. Source: Say\_what\_moves.docx (7 screens).

**Version 2.** Rewritten as a refresher that plays inside the LMS about two weeks after the course, followed by one check question. The film no longer assumes the viewer remembers the scenario.

## 1. Treatment

**Purpose.** Refresher, launched in the LMS about two weeks after learners finish the course. By then most people keep the gist ("ask before you say yes") and have lost the specifics: who Nadia is, who owns the harbour costing, what the four answers were. So the film rebuilds the situation from zero in its first 30 seconds, then makes the viewer pull the answer back out of memory before it shows it. It does not re-teach the course.

**Audience.** Office staff: project control, finance, sales support, planning. People whose work arrives as requests from several directions and whose shortcuts are invisible, because nothing on a desk visibly breaks.

**Playback context.** Inside the LMS, with sound, followed directly by one scenario question (section 7). Some viewers will still play it muted in an open-plan office, so every outcome, role and the key sentence also appear as on-screen text.

**Length: about 103 seconds.** The core story is still Screens 1 to 3 only. The extra time over version 1 goes to re-establishing the week plan and naming each person on screen, not to a second message. The follow-up stories from Screens 4 and 6 stay out and make a better second film (see the cut list).

**Behaviour change.** When new work lands on a full week, the person says which work gives way and asks the owner of that work before it moves.

**Controlling idea.** On a full week every yes moves someone else's work, so the owner of that work has to be asked before it moves, not told after.

**Shape: follow the object, with a recall pause and one rewind.** The film follows the harbour costing, drawn as a tile on a labelled week plan, from Thursday 15:00 to Nadia's "OK, go." The tile gives a body to a lesson that is otherwise invisible in an office: who knows what, and when. At the moment of the director's request the film freezes for three seconds on "What do you say?" That pause is short on purpose. It nudges recall, and the LMS question after the film does the real retrieval work. Two wrong answers play out, then the film rewinds to the right one.

**Hook.** Sound and image: an unlabelled tile shoved off a full rail, two beats of silence, a distant thud. The rail then rebuilds with labels, which turns the hook into the set-up. **Ending.** Callback to the first frame, with the opposite result. **Tone.** Wry. **VO.** Sparse narrator plus one in-scene character line.

## 2. Cut list

- **Screen 1, answers C ("No") and D ("Ask Nadia").** Both only make sense if you remember Nadia's role and her client meeting. The two "yes" answers are the most common real habits and need no back-story.
- **Screen 4, Nadia in the corridor ("Ideally both").** When the asker also owns the work that moves, the rule shifts. Strong material, different rule. Second film.
- **Screen 6, the week that still does not fit.** Every trade-off was agreed, nobody agreed to the total. Its own message, and the best spine for a second film, paired with Screen 4.
- **Screen 5, the ten-minute favour.** Estimating before promising is a separate skill. The sort rule repeats what the film already shows with the director.
- **Screen 7, the if-then plan and the email to the manager.** Personal reflection works in the course and dies on screen.
- **The Sarens review quote on naming priorities.** Abstract. The rail shows the same thing in two seconds.
- **Screen 3, option C (going to the director's manager).** A third path dilutes the puzzle-versus-plan comparison.
- **Screen 2 distractors (Friday admin, "later", "after the tender", asking the director).** The course teaches these. The film shows only the finished sentence.
- **Most of the exact times.** Kept only as on-screen clocks where they carry the story.

**Proposed second film:** "Nobody agreed to the total", 60 to 75 seconds, built from Screens 4 and 6. A countdown to Thursday's bridge report suits it.

## 4. VO notes

**Word count.** Narrator 109 words, controller line 27 words, total 136. At 2.3 words per second that is about 59 seconds of speech in a 103-second film. The opening, the recall pause, the rewind and the ending stay with picture and sound.

**Timed read per row.** 0:09 row: 10 words, about 4 s in 10 s, leaving time to read the labels. 0:19 row: 20 words, 9 s in 10 s. 0:32 row: 17 words, 7 s in 12 s, start after the first take is underway. 0:44 row: 16 words, 7 s in 8 s, read straight into the rewind. Controller line: 27 words, about 12 s in 15 s, conversational pace. 1:07 row: 11 words, 5 s in 9 s, pause after "Good." 1:16 row: 17 words, 7.5 s in 9 s. 1:25 row: 19 words, 8 s in 12 s.

**Narrator.** Dry, warm, unhurried. No coaching voice, no upward lift at line ends. Clear for non-native English listeners across Sarens regions: neutral accent, no fast contractions.

**Controller.** A second, different voice, cast to match the character design. Plain and calm, not rehearsed, with a small breath before "I'll check that".

**Recording note.** Record the controller line in one take, start to finish. The on-screen gaps are timed to its natural rhythm, so animation follows the read.

## 5. Animator notes

**Hero shape: the week rail.** A flat horizontal track with tiles sitting edge to edge. In the cold open the tiles are plain; from 0:09 on they carry labels and owner figures. Each tile carries its owner's colour and each owner wears it: harbour PM red, director a second accent, Nadia a third, the bridge PM a fourth, the controller's own admin neutral grey. The new request is always the white tile. Keep the rail the same length, height and tile size in every scene.

**Transitions.** Element-driven throughout. The fallen tile flying back up rebuilds the rail. The white tile swipes between the two takes. The rewind runs everything backwards on the rail rather than cutting. The second message bubble wipes the first.

**Sync points that must land exactly.**

- 0:03 the thud, after two clean beats of silence.
- 0:07 piano note on the title flip.
- 0:29 to 0:32 the freeze: music fully out, only the clock tick. Do not shorten below three seconds.
- 0:52 to 1:07 each bracket in the on-screen sentence clicks into place on the matching words of the controller's read.
- 1:33 the brighter chime on "OK, go."
- 1:40 the final click replaces the opening thud. Use the same slide sound so the callback reads.

**Muted check.** Watched without sound, the viewer still gets: who owns each tile, the request, the recall prompt, two labelled answers that go wrong, the finished sentence, the PM's reply, Nadia's role, the two messages with their replies, and the end line. Test a silent cut before sign-off.

## 7. LMS check after the film

One question, directly after the film. It uses a new situation with new people on purpose. Repeating the film's scenario would only test whether they watched; a new one tests whether they can use the rule.

**Wednesday 11:00. The fleet planner asks you for last quarter's crane utilisation figures by tomorrow afternoon. Tomorrow you are finishing the monthly report for the bridge job's project manager, and it will take the whole day. What do you say to the fleet planner?**

A. "Sure, I'll get it done tonight so the report doesn't slip." Feedback: The report keeps its date, but your evening pays for it and nobody sees the trade-off. The next request will arrive the same way.

B. "I can have them tomorrow afternoon if the bridge report moves to Friday. I'll check that with the bridge project manager and come back to you by 14:00." (Correct) Feedback: You named what moves and to which day, and you asked the one person who owns it before anything moved. The planner knows when the answer comes.

C. "I can have them tomorrow afternoon if the bridge report moves to Friday. Could you check with the bridge project manager if that's OK?" Feedback: The right person gets asked, but by the wrong one. The bridge PM hears about your trade-off second hand, from someone whose work is not on the line.

D. "Tomorrow is full, so please check with Nadia first." Feedback: Nadia does not own the bridge report, and she cannot see your week. The planner waits, and the bridge PM is still the last to know.
