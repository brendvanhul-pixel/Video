# Explainer script: Same page

Refresher film for Topic 4, Their view first · Nov 19, 2026 · @Brend

## Treatment

An 80-second refresher built on the nine o'clock call with Mira. It runs the call once with the engineer answering straight away with his view, freezes, rewinds, and runs it again with him saying her point back first.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Refresher, launched in the LMS about two weeks after Topic 4 |
| Audience | Project engineers, planners and other office roles who issue plans that site has to carry out |
| Playback | LMS, with sound, one check question straight after. Dialogue is on-screen speech bubbles, so the story holds when muted |
| Length | 80 seconds |
| Behaviour change | When you hear something you disagree with, say the other person's point back until they agree you have it, ask one short question, then give your view with your reason |

**Controlling idea.** When you disagree, say their point back until they agree you have it, and only then give your view with its reason.

**Shape: freeze and replay.** In both runs Mira finishes her sentence and the engineer gives his view. The only thing that changes is what comes before his view. A replay is the one shape that isolates that difference, because everything else in the frame stays the same.

**Image.** Two copies of the same lift plan, one on the quay and one on the engineer's screen. Mira's copy has the client's trucks on position two; his does not. In the first run his view bounces off the gap between the copies. In the replay, saying her point back moves the trucks onto his copy, she confirms, and the two copies slide together into one. His view then crosses without resistance. Getting on the same page is shown literally.

**The question** stays small: one line and one icon between the say-back and the view, with an on-screen label and no narration. It is there because it turns up the truck times, which make the later start possible.

**Tone.** Wry and quietly tense. The engineer's first reply is reasonable and well argued, which is why it works as a mirror.

**Refresher set-up.** By 0:17 on-screen labels have re-established who is involved, what each person owns and what is at stake. Nothing depends on remembering the course.

**Deviation from the course.** In S1 option A the engineer interrupts. Here he lets Mira finish in both runs, so viewers cannot read the lesson as "don't interrupt". The wrong move is giving his view before saying hers back.

**Known gap.** The "fix it fast before asking" habit (course option C) is not in the film. The LMS question covers it with one of its wrong answers.

## VO notes

63 words of narration, about 27.5 seconds at 2.3 words per second, in an 80-second film. Seven of eleven rows have no VO.

| Row | Words | Read time | Row length | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:06–0:17 | 18 | 7.8 s | 11 s | 3.2 s |
| 0:24–0:32 | 12 | 5.2 s | 8 s | 2.8 s |
| 0:39–0:48 | 17 | 7.4 s | 9 s | 1.6 s |
| 0:54–1:04 | 16 | 7.0 s | 10 s | 3.0 s |

The 0:39 and 0:54 rows carry both a speech bubble and VO. Start the VO only once the bubble has fully typed, otherwise viewers read and listen at the same time and lose both. If 0:39 runs long, cut "That isn't agreeing." and keep the rest. If the 0:54 bubble feels heavy, shorten it to "Then position two is on hold until I revise it, because your crew plans from this sequence."

**Voice.** One narrator, second person, talking to the engineer as a colleague who has been in this call, not as a trainer. Dry and unhurried at 0:24, plain and direct from 0:39 on. No uplift at the end of lines.

**Casting.** Neutral international English, mid-range, any gender. Avoid a strong regional accent: much of the audience hears English as a second language. Mira, the engineer and the project manager have no voices; their lines stay in bubbles, which keeps the film muted-safe and saves casting three actors.

**Do not voice** the on-screen labels ("Say it back.", "Ask.", "Your view, and why.") or the final rule. They land harder read than heard.

## Animator notes

**Hero shape: the lift plan sheet.** A flat sheet with a crane symbol and three position markers. It exists as two copies for most of the film: Mira's on the quay, the engineer's in the office, always slightly offset so the gap between them reads. The gap is where unheard views fall. When the copies lock into one at 0:46, that is the image of the film.

**The phone line** joins quay and office for the whole call. Bubbles travel along it. Mira's bubble is in the accent colour, the others neutral grey.

**Key transitions**

1. 0:00, one sheet peels into two copies. Keep it slow and quiet: the viewer should not yet know it matters.
2. 0:20, his first bubble drops into the gap between the copies. Same gap, same drop path as the copies' offset, so the cause is visible.
3. 0:39 to 0:46, his say-back bubble physically carries her trucks onto his copy; her check mark appears; only then do the copies slide together and lock. The order on screen is the order of the rule.
4. 0:54, his view crosses the locked sheet in one clean move, the reverse of the 0:20 drop.
5. 1:12, the end frame matches 0:00 in position and scale: one sheet, now carrying everything.

**Hard sync points**

- 0:46 paper click lands on the frame the copies lock.
- 0:35 to 0:38, three ticks fill the progress line; the line completes on the third tick.
- 1:09 four outrigger clunks on the music beat.

**Watch out for:** the trucks must not look like they belong to Sarens. Client colours, no Sarens livery. The project manager is busy, not a villain. The engineer's first reply must look calm and competent, not arrogant; the point is that a good argument still bounces when it comes first.

## LMS check question

One question, shown directly after the film. New people, new job, same rule: say it back until they agree, ask, then your view with your reason.

**Scenario.** You are the transport engineer who planned the route for an abnormal load convoy. The route is approved in the transport permit and you are confident in it. On Wednesday at 06:40, Joris, the convoy lead, calls from a lay-by: "The route won't work at the ring road junction. There's a barrier across the slip road." The convoy is due to move in twenty minutes.

**Stem.** What do you say to Joris?

| Option | Next | Feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. "The route is approved in the permit, so we drive it as planned." | Joris says "Fine" and the convoy leaves. It stops at the barrier at 07:20 with traffic building behind it. | Your reason was sound, but you gave it before you knew you and Joris were looking at the same road. |
| B. (correct) "So the slip road is closed and you can't get through? How long is the barrier there?" Then: "Hold in the lay-by, because the permit only covers the approved route. I'll check a later departure." | Joris confirms, and says it is a night roadworks crew packing up, gone by nine. The permit allows the later start and the convoy leaves at 09:15 on the approved route. | Saying it back got you both on the same road, the question found the time, and your view came with a reason he could act on. |
| C. "Understood. Hold there, I'll plan a new route and send it within the hour." | You spend the morning on a detour and a permit amendment. At 09:05 Joris mentions the barrier has just been taken away. | Holding the convoy was right, but you acted on a problem neither of you had checked. |
| D. "You're there, you know best. Take whatever way works." | Joris takes a detour that is not in the permit. The deviation is now something the client and the authorities can ask about. | Trusting the person on site is fair, but you gave no view at all, and the permit risk lands on Joris. |

**Closest distractor: A.** It is a view with a solid reason, which is exactly what the film asks for, only in the wrong place. The difference from B is the order, so it tests whether learners took the say-back or only the "give your reason" half. Flag for SME review that A reads as tempting and not as a straw man.

The stem no longer echoes the film's freeze-frame wording, so learners have to recognise the moment themselves.

**For SME check**

- Joris and the role "convoy lead" are invented. Confirm the right Sarens title for the person who would make this call, and that they would call the transport engineer rather than a planner or the transport manager.
- B assumes the transport engineer can tell a convoy to hold and can check a later departure against the permit. Permit rules on time windows vary by country.
- D's consequence is kept vague on purpose. If SMEs want a sharper one, it should match what actually happens in the main markets.
